Shenzhen · Lead story

Trump, Xi and Putin meet three-way for the first time

The three leaders held a closed-door trilateral session of about 80 minutes at Kylin Villa, Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit, after Trump met Putin and Xi separately. Afterwards China and Russia published a four-point “Declaration Towards Peace”: UN reform, more refined petroleum supply, limits on ICC overreach, and dialogue on reducing nuclear risks.

No Ukraine or Iran commitment, no arsenal cuts, no timetable, no petroleum figure. APEC leaders also adopted the “2026 APEC Leaders’ Shenzhen Outlook” on trade, data and AI (reported).

The Gulf

Trump weighs new strikes on Iran as Israel presses for a joint campaign

Trump is weighing new intelligence that Iran is rebuilding its missile and drone arsenals and advancing chemical and biological weapons programmes, and Israel’s prime minister is pressing the US to agree a joint 4–7-day airstrike campaign. Western officials say Trump has not yet decided; European and Gulf governments are lobbying against a repeat of the February–April campaign. Brent crude traded near $92.60 a barrel.

Reported

An American MQ-9 drone was reported shot down east of the Strait of Hormuz.

Ukraine

Kerch Bridge hit a third time in four days

Ukraine struck the Crimean Bridge overnight, Neptune missiles hitting the rail section near the arch. Russia suspended train traffic and reported no casualties. Rail service resumed that afternoon on one of the two tracks; the road span remains closed after the 17 November strike collapsed about 225 metres of roadway.

Berlin

Hours after the Shenzhen trilateral, European allies met in Berlin — Germany, France, Britain, Poland, Italy and NATO chief Mark Rutte — to work on legally binding security guarantees for Ukraine after a ceasefire. Britain committed 1,000 British-built long-range drones and fast attack boats with Norway, on top of a £10bn support package.

The Hague

ICJ: Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice ruled 9–6 that Myanmar committed genocide in its 2017 Rakhine crackdown — the first time in the court’s 80-year history it has held a state responsible for genocide. The court estimated about 10,000 Rohingya killed and 392 villages burned in whole or part, and ordered reparations and a safe, voluntary, dignified return of refugees.

Climate · COP31

Draft deal drops fossil-fuel phaseout and $120bn finance goal

A slimmed-down revised draft published in Antalya stripped out all reference to ending fossil-fuel use — Monday’s draft had two — and dropped a $120bn adaptation-finance target, ahead of Friday’s deadline. It would be the first COP cover decision to explicitly acknowledge warming is projected to exceed 1.5°C.

GTA VI is out

Grand Theft Auto VI launched on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, 13 years after GTA V. Standard Edition $79.99, Ultimate $99.99; no PC version announced.

Markets

Anthropic debuts on Nasdaq

Anthropic began trading under ticker ANT after pricing its IPO at $83 a share, above the $70–$78 marketed range, raising about $81.3bn — the largest IPO on record by the most-cited comparison. Shares were reported to open at $111.50.

Gaza

Israel ends Gaza ground raid; road reopens

Israel’s army said its ground operation in central Gaza was completed and the Salah al-Din road reopened; families returned to Nuseirat camp. The raid, launched Wednesday, was the first publicly announced ground operation west of the ceasefire “Yellow Line” since the October 2025 truce.
