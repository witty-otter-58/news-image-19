## The stories on the page

- **Lead, Shenzhen:** Trump, Xi and Putin held their first-ever three-way
  meeting — a closed-door session of about 80 minutes at Kylin Villa on the
  sidelines of the APEC summit. China and Russia then published a four-point
  "Declaration Towards Peace": UN reform, more refined petroleum supply,
  limits on ICC overreach, and dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. No Ukraine
  or Iran commitment, no arsenal cuts, no timetable, no petroleum figure.
- **The Gulf:** Trump is weighing new intelligence that Iran is rebuilding
  its missile and drone arsenals and advancing chemical and biological weapons
  programmes; Israel's prime minister is pressing for a joint 4–7-day
  airstrike campaign. Trump has not yet decided; European and Gulf governments
  are lobbying against a repeat of the February–April campaign. Brent crude
  near $92.60 a barrel.
- **Ukraine:** Kerch Bridge hit a third time in four days — Neptune missiles
  struck the rail section near the arch overnight. Trains resumed that
  afternoon on one of two tracks; the road span stays closed after the
  17 November strike collapsed about 225 metres of roadway. In Berlin, hours
  after the trilateral, European allies worked on legally binding security
  guarantees for Ukraine; Britain committed 1,000 British-built long-range
  drones and fast attack boats with Norway, on top of a £10bn package.
- **The Hague:** The ICJ ruled 9–6 that Myanmar committed genocide against
  the Rohingya in its 2017 Rakhine crackdown — the first time in the court's
  80-year history it has held a state responsible for genocide. About 10,000
  killed, 392 villages burned; reparations and a safe, voluntary, dignified
  return of refugees ordered.
- **Climate, COP31:** A revised draft deal in Antalya stripped out all
  reference to ending fossil-fuel use and dropped a $120bn adaptation-finance
  target, ahead of Friday's deadline. It would be the first COP cover decision
  to explicitly acknowledge warming is projected to exceed 1.5°C.
- **Culture:** GTA VI launched on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, 13 years
  after GTA V. Standard Edition $79.99, Ultimate $99.99; no PC version
  announced.
- **Markets:** Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq under ticker ANT after
  pricing its IPO at $83 a share — above the $70–$78 marketed range — raising
  about $81.3bn, the largest IPO on record by the most-cited comparison.
- **Gaza:** Israel's army said its ground operation in central Gaza was
  completed and the Salah al-Din road reopened; families returned to Nuseirat
  camp. The raid was the first publicly announced ground operation west of the
  ceasefire "Yellow Line" since the October 2025 truce.
- **Also today:** Cyclone Akvan in the Bay of Bengal, landfall forecast
  between Narsapur and Puri by noon Friday at 90–100 km/h gusting to 110;
  the Awami League nationwide lockdown took effect in Bangladesh; Denmark's
  parliament voted 109–0 to approve the US–Denmark–Greenland security
  agreement.
- **Reported, single source:** an American MQ-9 drone shot down east of the
  Strait of Hormuz; the APEC leaders' Shenzhen Outlook adopted; IMF chief
  Georgieva warning worldwide inflation has hit 4.7%; Anthropic shares
  opening at $111.50.
